Arbor

A financial resilience and wealth modeling platform helping individuals, families, and businesses understand stability, analyze risk, and model future financial outcomes.

The Problem

Most personal finance applications focus on recording transactions and monitoring spending.

They rarely answer deeper questions:

  • How financially resilient am I?
  • What happens if my income falls?
  • How long can I withstand unexpected expenses?
  • Am I genuinely financially secure?

Arbor was designed to bridge this gap by combining financial tracking, resilience measurement, risk analysis, and future projections into a single platform.

Why I Built Arbor

During my internship at Calcutta Star Exports, I observed how difficult it was for both individuals and businesses to gain a clear picture of their financial position beyond spreadsheets and transaction records.

I noticed a similar challenge at home, where understanding long-term financial health often required significant manual effort. Inspired by concepts from economics, finance, and quantitative modeling, I began building Arbor to focus not on spending, but on financial resilience.

Financial Resilience Index (FRI)

The Financial Resilience Index is the intellectual core of Arbor.

It combines savings strength, debt sustainability, emergency coverage, and expense stability into a single resilience score ranging from 0–100.

Similar to how a credit score measures borrowing reliability, the FRI measures financial resilience.

Monte Carlo Simulation Engine

Arbor models thousands of potential financial futures rather than relying on a single forecast.

Variables include:

  • Income Growth
  • Expense Inflation
  • Investment Returns
  • Revenue Variability
  • Financial Shocks

This enables users to evaluate uncertainty, probability distributions, and future risk.

Shock Modeling Framework

  • Income Shock Simulation
  • Expense Shock Simulation
  • Inflation Shock Modeling
  • Business Revenue Shock Analysis

These simulations help users understand how resilient they remain under adverse conditions.
