Becoming Editor-in-Chief

For a long time, I was just a contributor.

Writing, editing, and being part of Fiscal Focus — my school’s financial literacy magazine — was something I genuinely enjoyed.

But now, as I step into my senior year, that role has changed.

I’ve been given the responsibility of leading the magazine as Editor-in-Chief.

It’s something I’ve wanted for years.

Since grade 9, I’ve seen this position as more than just a title. It represents ownership — not just over content, but over direction.

Fiscal Focus played an important role in shaping my interest in economics.

It wasn’t a single defining moment that pushed me toward the subject, but it was one of the environments that made me take it seriously.

Writing, reading, and thinking about real-world financial ideas made economics feel less like a subject and more like something worth exploring deeply.

And now, being in a position to shape that experience for others feels significant.

This role comes with responsibility.

The quality of the magazine, the ideas we publish, and the way we present them — all of it now depends on the decisions I make.

It also comes with expectation.

I’m not just continuing something. I’m expected to improve it.

That’s what makes it challenging.

And that’s what makes it meaningful.

Right now, the goal is simple:

To make every edition better than the last.

Not just in terms of content, but in clarity, depth, and relevance.

Because if the magazine influenced me in any way, I want it to do the same for others.

And now, I have the opportunity to make that happen.
