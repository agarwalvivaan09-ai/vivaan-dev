How I Think About Exams

Exams are usually treated as a test of intelligence.

I don’t think they are.

They’re a test of consistency — and more importantly, a test of how well you can handle pressure when it actually matters.

I’ve gone through both extremes.

There have been phases where I’ve studied consistently, planned things out, and felt in control. And there have been phases where I’ve relied on last-minute effort, trying to compensate in a few days for what should have been done over months.

The difference in outcome is obvious.

Last-minute preparation always feels intense, but it’s unstable. You might get through it once, maybe twice, but it’s not something you can depend on.

What actually works is much simpler — showing up every day, even when you don’t feel like it.

That’s the part no one talks about.

Motivation doesn’t last. Discipline does.

And most of the time, it’s not about doing something extraordinary. It’s about not breaking the system you’ve built.

Pressure is another thing people misunderstand.

Before exams, there’s always some level of stress — expectations, uncertainty, the fear of underperforming.

Earlier, I used to see that as something negative.

Now, I see it differently.

Pressure is useful — if you’re prepared.

It sharpens your focus, makes you more aware, and forces you to take things seriously.

But if your preparation is weak, that same pressure turns into panic.

And panic destroys performance.

I’ve felt both.

Walking into an exam knowing you’ve covered everything feels completely different from walking in hoping the paper is easy.

One gives you control. The other takes it away.

Another mistake I used to make was overcomplicating things.

Too many resources, too many strategies, too many plans.

It feels productive, but it isn’t.

What actually works is doing fewer things properly.

Depth over variety.

Understanding over memorizing.

And repeating the basics until they become automatic.

Exams don’t reward how much you know.

They reward how clearly you can apply what you know under time pressure.

And that’s a different skill altogether.

At the same time, I don’t think exams define everything.

They matter — but only to an extent.

What they really test is your ability to stay consistent, manage time, and perform under pressure.

And those are skills that go far beyond academics.

So I don’t see exams as something to “get through” anymore.

I see them as a system.

And if the system works, the results usually follow.
