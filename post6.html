My First Crisis as CEO

Every organization eventually faces a moment that tests it.

For me, that moment came through something much smaller than I expected — a disagreement over finances.

There was a miscommunication between me and my CFO regarding how certain financial decisions were being handled.

On its own, it wasn’t a major issue.

But the way I handled it turned it into one.

Instead of addressing the issue privately, I confronted him publicly.

In that moment, I wasn’t thinking as a leader. I was reacting.

And that made things worse.

What could have been a simple conversation became a visible conflict. It created tension, affected the team, and ultimately led to him stepping away from his role.

That was my first real failure as a leader.

But it didn’t end there.

After things settled, I reached out to him privately. We spoke, cleared the misunderstanding, and I apologized.

And that conversation taught me more than the conflict itself.

The first lesson was simple: never make a private issue public.

Whether it’s a co-founder or a team member, problems should be resolved directly, not in front of others.

Public confrontation doesn’t solve problems — it amplifies them.

The second lesson was about unity.

As founders, you have to stand together. Even if there are disagreements internally, the outside view should always be aligned.

The moment leadership appears divided, it creates instability. People begin to take sides. And that can weaken the entire organization.

The third lesson was personal.

Not every disagreement is worth damaging a relationship over.

Some friendships matter more than being right in the moment.

Holding grudges doesn’t solve anything. Honest conversations do.

And most of the time, people are willing to understand — if you’re willing to talk.

Looking back, I could have handled the situation very differently.

But mistakes like this are what shape your approach going forward.

Leadership isn’t just about making decisions.

It’s about knowing how to handle people — especially when things go wrong.
