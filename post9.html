After Our Third Fundraiser

Our third fundraiser, Football Fiesta, was supposed to be straightforward.

It was a football tournament — teams competing for a cash prize. Everything had been planned carefully. Registrations were done, teams were verified, and the structure was in place.

Until the day of the event.

During final verification, one team stood out. They looked older than expected. When we asked for identification, something didn’t feel right.

On closer inspection, their documents didn’t check out. When we tried to verify them digitally, it became clear that the IDs were not valid.

That’s when the situation escalated.

They refused to leave. They started arguing. At one point, they disrupted the event entirely, sitting on the turf and preventing the tournament from continuing.

Everything stopped.

For a few moments, I was completely overwhelmed.

This wasn’t something you prepare for. There’s no checklist for situations like this.

But in that moment, I realized something important:

if I didn’t take control, the entire event would collapse.

I stepped in, handled the situation directly, and focused on resolving it as quickly as possible.

It wasn’t perfect. It wasn’t clean.

But eventually, the situation was contained and the event continued.

Looking back, this experience taught me a few important things.

First, preparation isn’t just about planning the ideal scenario.

It’s about being ready for the unexpected — especially the things you can’t fully control.

Second, leadership is tested in moments of uncertainty.

When things are going well, anyone can lead. When things break down, people look for direction.

And in those moments, hesitation makes things worse.

You don’t always have time for perfect decisions. Sometimes you just need to act.

Finally, I learned that composure matters.

Not because you always feel calm — but because you need to appear in control, even when you’re not.

Because the moment people sense uncertainty, the situation can spiral further.

Football Fiesta wasn’t just another event.

It was a reminder that leadership isn’t defined by smooth execution — but by how you handle disruption.
